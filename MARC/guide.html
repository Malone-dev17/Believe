1. Opening M.A.R.C

  1. Double-click the M.A.R.C icon on your desktop. It starts quietly in the background and opens in your browser. Double-clicking again only reopens the page.
  2. Bookmark the page (Ctrl+D): http://127.0.0.1:8765/MARC/
  3. After restarting your laptop, double-click the icon once to start M.A.R.C again.

The online copy on GitHub Pages only shows the layout. Your jobs, CVs, tracker and research stay on your laptop and only appear when you open M.A.R.C from the icon.

Target: 30–50 strong applications a day. Quality beats volume. Check every advert before approving, because alert emails only give the job title, so scoring has less to go on.

Let Claude fill in the forms (assisted apply)

With Chrome connected, say start applying in a Claude session. For each approved role on an employer's own careers site (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever and similar), Claude opens the form, fills in your details, uploads the tailored CV and pastes the cover note, then stops. You check it and click Submit. Tell Claude when it's done and it marks the role applied. Sign-ins, CAPTCHAs and new accounts are always yours. LinkedIn, Indeed and Totaljobs applications stay manual, because those sites ban automation.

Found a job yourself? Click Add a job link in Job Hunter, paste the link, title, company and description, and M.A.R.C scores it and prepares the CV and note.

3. Your weekly routine

  • Monday about 9am (automatic): 3 new research cards on individual stocks, plus a news check on your YES watchlist.
  • Monday, 10 min: open Investments, read each card, and click YES (add to watchlist, with a reason) or NO. Everything you said yes to is under Watchlist.
  • Any day: look at the Tracker's weekly totals and response rate. If the response rate stays low, ask Claude to review which CV versions and roles are getting replies.

Research cards are facts with dated sources, not advice. M.A.R.C never places trades, and its Trading 212 key is read-only. Every card compares the investment's largest past fall with your 20% limit.

4. Things you can ask Claude

Open the Claude app, start a session in the Documents\command-centre folder, and type requests in plain English. For example:

Say thisWhat happens
import my job alertsRuns the alert import now instead of waiting for 8am.
add this job: <link> + paste the descriptionAdds, scores and prepares one job.
research <ticker>One research card, e.g. research TSLA.
research new investment ideasAn extra batch of cards outside Monday's run.
add <fact> to my CVUpdates your master CV and rebuilds every version. Only true facts: Claude won't invent experience.
add a target role: <title>Adds a role, with keywords and a CV version.
change my salary floor to £XChanges the scoring rules.
write a follow-up to <company>A tailored follow-up message (cold-email skill).
how is my job search going?A summary from the Tracker: totals, response rate, which CV versions get replies.

6. Adding skills later

A skill is a set of instructions that teaches Claude a new task. Every skill gets the same review-then-install treatment:

  1. Find a skill on GitHub (a folder with a SKILL.md file).
  2. In a Claude session in Documents\command-centre, paste the link and say review this skill.
  3. Claude reads every file and tells you in plain English what it does, what it needs access to, and anything risky or unnecessary.
  4. If you're happy, say install it. Claude copies it into .claude\skills\, leaves out anything unsafe (as it did with context-engineering's pre-approved commands), and tests that it works.
  5. Claude adds it to the Tools & Skills list in M.A.R.C, with what it's for and where it came from, and saves it to GitHub.

Warning signs Claude checks for (and you can too)

  • Asks for API keys or accounts, or sends your data to an outside service
  • Turns on tracking by default (browser-use did, so it was skipped)
  • Pre-approves commands, so Claude would stop asking your permission
  • Instructions that tell Claude to recommend the skill's own product

Plugins

Plugins bundle several skills. When Claude suggests one, an install card appears in the chat. Read the review first, then install from the card if you want it. To remove a skill, say remove the <name> skill.

7. Keeping your data safe

  • Everything personal lives in private\: your CV, profile, jobs, research, and keys. Git ignores that folder, so it never goes to GitHub, even though your repo is public.
  • private\keys.env holds your Trading 212 key. Never paste keys into chat, email or GitHub. If a key leaks, delete it in Trading 212 (Settings → API) and create a new read-only one.
  • M.A.R.C's server only answers your own laptop (127.0.0.1). Other websites can't read or change your data.
  • Back up private\ now and then: copy it to a USB stick or a private cloud folder (not GitHub).
